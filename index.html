I · The Letter

An Invitation, Extended

You have been considered, and your name has been placed forward. What follows is not a solicitation — it is an introduction to a small society of individuals who treat capital as a discipline rather than a pursuit.

The Cabin is not open to the public. There are no advertisements, no public campaigns, no outreach to strangers. Every seat is filled through the quiet recommendation of those already inside. You are reading this because someone you know believed you would understand what we are building — and why we build it this way.

What we offer is not novel in concept, only in execution: a structure by which disciplined participants compound their capital together, on short cycles, under terms that reward reliability and quietly exclude those who lack it. The mechanism is simple. The conduct required is not.

If what follows resonates, you may place your name on the waitlist. Should a seat open, you will be considered. If it does not resonate, no further action is required — the invitation simply expires.

— The Founding Circle Stewards of the Cabin
II · The Principles

Four Tenets That Govern the Cabin

These are not aspirations. They are the conditions of membership.

I

Compounding Discipline

Capital left in place grows faster than capital withdrawn. Members commit to leaving their stake in the pool — not for a season, but as a habit.

II

Short Cycles, Repeated

Capital is deployed in short, discrete cycles rather than long speculative positions. Velocity, not duration, produces the compound curve.

III

Collective Pooling

No single member carries a loan alone. Capital is drawn from the collective, returned to the collective, and the gains are shared by share.

IV

Merit, Not Size

Standing in the Cabin is earned through reliability, not through the size of one's contribution. A disciplined small member outranks an undisciplined large one.

III · The Mechanism

How the Cabin Operates

The structure is straightforward. The discipline required to sustain it is not.

I

Capital Is Pledged

Members contribute capital into a collective pool. Each member receives shares, priced at the rate prevailing at the moment they enter.

II

Cycles Are Run

The pool deploys capital as short-term loans to vetted borrowers, each carrying a small return — a tip — paid back at cycle close.

III

Tips Are Distributed

Returns are credited to participating members, weighted by the shares they held when the cycle began. The pool grows.

IV

Shares Appreciate

As tips accumulate in the pool, each share becomes worth more. Later entrants pay a higher price — early discipline is rewarded.

V · The Moral Framework

What the Cabin Requires

Membership is not a transaction. It is a stewardship — of capital, of reputation, of trust.

Character, Before Capital

An invitation is extended only after the founding circle has personally considered the candidate's conduct, discretion, and reliability. A larger contribution does not substitute for an unproven character.

Discretion Is Required

Members do not discuss the Cabin's operations, its participants, or its borrowers outside the circle. What happens in the Cabin stays in the Cabin — not as secrecy for its own sake, but as the condition that makes trust possible.

Honor Your Commitments

Capital pledged to the pool remains in the pool. Members who withdraw without cause forfeit standing. The compound curve depends on discipline, and discipline depends on each member's word.

Vouch for Those You Bring

A member who recommends a candidate vouches for them — personally. If the candidate proves unsuitable, the recommending member's standing is affected. This is how the Cabin keeps its standards without bureaucracy.

I

Application Received

Your name and a brief note are placed on the waitlist. No financial information is required at this stage — only your name, and the member who referred you.

II

The Circle Reviews

The founding circle reviews each candidate against the moral framework. This is not a credit check. It is a character consideration, conducted quietly and without ceremony.

III

A Seat Opens

When a seat becomes available, the most suitable candidate on the waitlist is approached privately. The terms of entry — share price, minimum stake, conduct expected — are extended in writing.

IV

The Pledge

If accepted, the candidate pledges capital and receives shares. A sponsor — the member who referred them — stands as guarantor of their first cycle. From that point, they are inside.

VII · The Cabin

What Membership Provides

What you receive by joining — beyond the return on your capital.

Compounding Capital

Your stake participates in every cycle the pool runs. Returns are credited by share, and shares appreciate as the pool grows. Left in place, the curve compounds.

Shared Risk

No single member carries a loan alone. Each cycle's deployment is distributed across the pool. Defaults, when they occur, are absorbed collectively — never by one participant.

A Trusted Circle

The members around you have been vetted by the same standard you were. Their conduct reflects on the Cabin, and the Cabin's conduct reflects on them. The result is a network that holds its shape.

Lender Protection

For larger positions, the Cabin offers optional protection — a fee paid at funding that returns a portion of principal if a borrower defaults. Members choose per-loan which positions to protect.

Full Ledger Transparency

Every loan, every cycle, every adjustment is recorded in a permanent ledger. Members see the same numbers administrators see. There are no private books.

A Voice in the Cabin

Standing in the Cabin is earned by conduct, not by share count. A disciplined small member's voice carries the same weight as a large one's, when the circle convenes.

VIII · Considered Questions

What You May Wish to Know

Is there a minimum contribution to join?
There is a minimum stake, set at the founding circle's discretion. It is not large — the Cabin values discipline over deposit size. The exact figure is communicated privately when a seat is offered. Members may increase their stake over time at the prevailing share price.
How are returns actually paid to me?
Returns are credited to your member account as each cycle closes, distributed by your share of the pool at that cycle's start. They remain in the pool and compound unless you formally request a withdrawal. The Cabin encourages members to leave returns in place — the compound curve depends on it.
Can I withdraw my capital if I need to?
Yes — but with conditions. Withdrawals are processed at cycle boundaries, not mid-cycle. Early withdrawal within the first cycle of membership is not permitted. Members who withdraw without intention to return forfeit their standing, and a future return would require a fresh invitation.
What happens if a borrower defaults on a loan I participated in?
The loss is shared across all participants of that loan, weighted by share. If you purchased Lender Protection on the loan, a portion of your principal is recovered into a separate credit balance, which is then redeployed on your behalf in future loans. The Cabin's structure is designed so that no single default materially damages any single member.
How is the Cabin governed? Who makes decisions?
Day-to-day operations are run by an administrator appointed by the founding circle. Material changes — to the moral framework, the seat count, the cycle structure — are decided by the founding circle collectively. In disputes between members, the founding circle's ruling is final.
Is the Cabin regulated or insured?
The Cabin is a private members' collective, not a regulated financial institution. Capital pledged to the pool is not insured by any government body. Membership is extended only to those who understand and accept this condition. If you require regulatory protection, the Cabin is not for you.
What disqualifies a member from the Cabin?
Breach of discretion, failure to honor commitments, conduct unbecoming of a steward, or vouching for a candidate who proves unworthy. The founding circle may, at its discretion, remove a member and return their capital at the prevailing share price. Expulsion is rare, but it is final.
How long does it take for a waitlist candidate to be considered?
It depends on seat availability. The Cabin holds 25 seats. When one opens, the waitlist is reviewed and the most suitable candidate is approached. Some candidates are considered within weeks; others wait months. The Cabin does not rush this process. Patience is itself a quality we observe.
